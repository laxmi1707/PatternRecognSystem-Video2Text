All figures are out-of-fold: every recording is scored by models that never saw it. Steps are counted after repeated reads are merged, one-to-one against the log. Definitions are at the end of the page.

How it works

Two stages run on the same extracted frames. The first decides which application is on screen. The second finds candidate text and scores it with a model trained on that application, because where an input field sits on screen is the strongest clue it has, and that differs from one application to the next.

Seeing typing without a keystroke log

The extractor tracks every OCR region by where it sits on screen. When the text at one position gets longer from one keyframe to the next, something is being typed there. This works in a spreadsheet's formula bar as well as at a shell prompt, which a rule that looked for $ never could.

Where steps are lost

Almost every typed string is somewhere in the candidate set. Most of the loss happens when the ranker decides what to keep, so the filter, not the extractor, is where further work should go.

Results

Six measurements, each out of fold. The first answers whether naming the application first is worth it; the rest show where the detector works and where it stops.

How this was measured

  • Data. VideoCUA screen recordings with their action logs: 6,991 recordings in 70 applications. The 61 applications with at least five logged typed strings have a stage-2 model.
  • Folds. Five folds grouped by recording, shared by both stages, so a recording's application guess and the ranker applied to it both come from models that never saw it.
  • A step. A logged typed string of at least three letters or digits. Shorter keystrokes such as 1 or ok are not instructions a reader can follow.
  • A match. Lowercase letters and digits only; one string contains the other, or they are at least 80% similar, which tolerates OCR misreads.
  • Precision is the share of SOP steps that claim a logged string; recall is the share of logged strings claimed by a step. Each step claims at most one string and each string is claimed once.
  • Not measured. Whether a human finds a step useful; applications, themes or versions outside the dataset; clicks of any kind.
